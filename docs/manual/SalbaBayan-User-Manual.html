SalbaBayan is a flood and storm warning application for one barangay. It carries the barangay's storm signal, its evacuation routes, its hazard reports and its rescue requests, and it is built to keep working when the mobile network does not.

1 · Getting started

SalbaBayan is a website that behaves like an app. There is nothing to buy, no account to create and no password to remember.

Opening it

Open salba-bayan.vercel.app in the phone's browser. The app opens straight away as a resident of Barangay #5 Callaguip. It does not ask for a name, an email address or a telephone number.

Installing it on the phone

Install it so that it opens from the home screen like any other app, and so that the phone keeps a copy for when there is no signal.

  • Android (Chrome): menu ⋮ → Add to Home screen or Install app.
  • iPhone (Safari): the share button → Add to Home Screen.
Do this before the storm

The app saves the barangay's map, routes and wording onto the phone the first time it is opened with a connection. Installing it during a storm, on a network that is already struggling, is the one time it may not work.

Language

The language button sits at the top of every screen, marked EN. English, Filipino (Tagalog) and Cebuano are written by people. The rest are machine-translated and labelled as such on the picker; a screen shown in one of those will say so, and falls back to Tagalog where a phrase is missing.

Ilocano is currently machine-translated. A native speaker should review it before the barangay relies on it.

Day and night

The button beside the language switch turns the dark screen on and off. Dark is for reading outdoors at night, and uses less battery on most phones. The choice is remembered on that phone.

The device code

Every phone is given a short code such as SB-0142, shown on the ME screen. It identifies the phone without identifying the person. It is what an official needs in order to make the phone a volunteer's or an official's, and it is what a volunteer scans at an evacuation centre.

2 · Who is who

Three roles. A phone has exactly one, and it is granted by an official — never chosen by the person holding the phone.

RoleWhat that phone can do
Resident Read the signal and what to do. See the map, the hazards, the flooding and the route to the nearest evacuation centre. Report a hazard or a flood depth. Press SOS. This is what every phone is until it is given something else.
Volunteer Everything a resident can do, plus: the rescue queue, taking a call and being routed to it, scanning residents into an evacuation centre, and keeping the headcount.
Official Everything a volunteer can do, plus: the dashboard, changing the barangay's advisory, placing hazards and flood readings on the map, clearing flood readings, managing evacuation centres and readiness.

Giving a phone a role

  1. The person opens ME and reads out their device code.
  2. An official with full access opens USER MANAGEMENT, searches for that code, chooses the role and presses and holds to confirm.
  3. The role arrives on that phone at its next sync — usually within seconds.

Opening the official pages

Official screens are reached at /lgu and ask for the barangay's 4-digit PIN each time the app is opened fresh. The PIN alone is not enough: a phone that has not been made an official in User Management cannot open those pages whatever it types.

There is also an access code, which grants full access on any phone. It exists so the barangay is never locked out of its own system, and it should be treated like the key to the hall.

Before residents use this

Change the PIN and the access code from the ones the system was set up with, in USER MANAGEMENT → ACCESS SETTINGS. Once the real officials hold the role on their own phones, consider switching the access code off entirely; it can be switched back on when it is needed.

Names

A resident may enter their name on ME. It is needed to file a hazard or flood report, and it is shown only to volunteers and officials — never to other residents. The SOS button never asks for it.

3 · Working without a signal

A storm takes the mobile network with it. The app is built for that, and it says plainly what it knows and what it has not yet been able to send.

The strip at the top

It saysIt means
SYNCED JUST NOWThe phone reached the barangay's records a moment ago. What is on screen is current.
CACHED 2 HOURS AGONo connection. What is on screen is what was last received, and it is two hours old.
2 WAITING TO SENDTwo things done on this phone — a report, an SOS, a headcount — are saved on the phone and have not reached the barangay yet. They go automatically when the connection returns.
1 FAILED TO SENDSomething was refused by the barangay's records. Open it and read the reason; it is usually a permission that has changed.

What works with no signal

  • The signal level, what to do, and the leave-before time.
  • The map, the barangay outline, the streets and the walking route to the evacuation centre.
  • The SOS button, the report screens, the headcount and the check-in scanner.
  • The documents saved on the readiness screen.

What does not

  • Anything new from other people — a hazard someone else has just reported, or a signal an official has just issued. The app shows what it last received and says how old it is.
  • The coloured street map, if the phone has never once loaded it. The barangay's own drawn map is used instead, and it holds every street and route.
  • The PAGASA bulletin on the official dashboard, which is read from PAGASA over the network.
The rule the app follows

Nothing on the screen ever claims to be current when it is not. An SOS raised with no signal is accepted, timed from the moment of the press, and sent when the network returns — and until then it says it is waiting.

4 · For residents

ResidentVolunteerOfficial

Five buttons along the bottom: HOME, MAP, SOS, REPORT, ME.

HOME — the signal and what to do

The number the barangay has issued, what it means for your street, and — from Signal 3 upwards — the time you must leave before, counting down.

No.What the app tells residents to do
0No active signal. Nothing has been issued.
1Prepare your go-bag. Watch for the next bulletin.
2Secure your home. Prepare your family to evacuate.
3Go to your evacuation centre now. Do not wait for dark.
4Conditions outside are dangerous. Leave now.
5Do not go outside. You should already be at the centre.

The wording shown is the barangay's instruction for your purok at that signal, so two streets may be told different things at the same signal.

MAP — where to go, and what is in the way

The map opens on the barangay with a green line drawn from your street to the nearest evacuation centre, along streets a person can actually walk. Below it: the distance, the walking time, and the next turn.

  • Pins. Red and amber are hazards, blue is flooding, green is an evacuation centre. Tap any pin to read what it is, when it was reported and where.
  • A faded pin means the report has no exact location, only a street. A faded flood reading also means it is more than three hours old — water moves.
  • AVOID above the map means a hazard sits on your route. The route is drawn around it where the streets allow.
  • The crosshair button returns the map to where you are.

REPORT — a hazard or a flood

  1. Choose what you saw: flood, tree, road, power, or other.
  2. For a flood, choose the depth against your own body — knee, waist, chest, above head — and name the street.
  3. For anything else, describe it and add a photo if you can.
  4. Tick use my current location only if you are standing at the place. Leave it off if you are reporting from somewhere else, and the street you name will be used.
  5. Send. It is saved on the phone first, so it is never lost if the network is down.

Your report appears on everyone's map — residents, volunteers and officials alike. Tap any row in the list below the form, including a flood reading, to see it on the map.

SOS — calling for rescue

One press. No login, no form, no waiting for GPS.

  • The screen then shows SENT, and changes to SEEN BY THE BARANGAY when a volunteer or official has acknowledged it — with the time you have been waiting, counting up.
  • With no signal it is accepted anyway, timed from the press, and sent automatically when the connection returns.
  • With no GPS fix your purok is sent instead, so responders still know which street to search.
  • To cancel, press and hold. A single tap cannot cancel a rescue request.
If nobody comes

SalbaBayan alerts the barangay's own volunteers and officials. It does not call the fire service, the police or the Coast Guard. In a life-threatening emergency, use the telephone as well.

5 · For volunteers

ResidentVolunteerOfficial

The bottom bar changes: HOME, SCAN, RESCUE, COUNT, ME. Everything a resident can do is still there.

RESCUE — the queue and the map

A full-screen map of the barangay with every open rescue request on it. Waiting calls pulse; that is the only thing on the map that moves.

  • The hamburger button at the top right opens the queue, oldest call first, with how long each has been waiting. The number beside it is how many are open.
  • Answering a call marks it seen for everyone, and draws your own walking route to it from where you are. The map then keeps both you and the call in view.
  • More than one of you may take the same call. Each phone draws its own route from its own position, and nobody sees anyone else's.
  • Marking it rescued closes it for everyone and clears the route from every phone that was going.
  • Hazards and flood readings are on this map too, so a route around a blocked road can be seen before setting out.

SCAN — checking residents into a centre

Point the camera at a resident's QR card, or type the code from it such as SB-0142. Then log what is true of them:

  • HERE — they are at this centre.
  • MOVED ON — they have left for somewhere else.
  • NEEDS HELP — flag them as a priority.
  • Priorities — medical, elderly, infant — may be added so the hall knows who needs attention first.

If the camera cannot be used, manual entry does the same job. Scanning works with no signal; the scans are held and sent when the connection returns.

COUNT — how many are inside

Choose the centre, then add people as they come in — one at a time, or a family together with the bulk count. The screen shows how many are inside, the capacity, and how many spaces are left, turning to FILLING UP and then FULL — DIRECT PEOPLE ELSEWHERE.

The ledger is append-only

Entries are never erased. A mistake is corrected by adding a correction, so the record of what was counted, when and by whom always survives. Two volunteers counting at the same door at the same time cannot overwrite each other.

6 · For officials

ResidentVolunteerOfficial

The dashboard is a full-screen map of the barangay with everything on it, and a sidebar opened from the menu button.

The map

  • Tap any street to drop a pin and choose what it is: a hazard, a flood depth, or a new evacuation centre. The pin is saved only after a press and hold.
  • Tap any existing pin to read it in the sidebar and act on it.
  • The counts at the top left — SOS, hazards, flood readings — stay visible while the sidebar is shut.

Changing the advisory

  1. Open CHANGE ADVISORY in the sidebar.
  2. Choose the level, and fill in the storm name, bulletin number and wind speed.
  3. At Signal 3 and above a leave-before time is required, and must be in the future.
  4. Check the preview — it is drawn with the same components residents will see.
  5. Press and hold to issue it.

Until the change reaches the barangay's records, the screen says NOT SENT YET and tells you to use the megaphone or the radio in the meantime. It says SENT when residents have actually been told, and REFUSED if it was rejected.

The PAGASA bulletin

The dashboard reads PAGASA's tropical cyclone bulletin and shows the storm, the bulletin number, the issue time, the winds, and the wind signal that covers this barangay. It always states when it was read and links to the bulletin itself.

  • USE THIS BULLETIN fills the advisory form with those values. It does not issue anything: you still read every field and press and hold, exactly as if you had typed them.
  • If PAGASA lists only part of the province — "the northern portion of Ilocos Norte" — the card shows the number but leaves the level alone and asks you to read the bulletin. Batac is not in every part of Ilocos Norte.
  • If the card says the bulletin could not be read, read it at PAGASA and set the advisory by hand. It is a reading aid; it is never the authority.
The bulletin is not an instruction

PAGASA's wind signal covers a province. The barangay's signal is a decision about this barangay, made by an official who knows which streets flood first. Nothing reaches residents that an official has not confirmed.

Evacuation centres

Up to three. Each has a name and a capacity, and each must sit inside the barangay. Residents are routed to whichever is nearest to their street, so adding or moving one re-routes the map for everyone. The barangay must keep at least one.

Flood readings

Only officials may clear a reading. Placing a new reading on the same spot clears the older ones there, so the map shows what the water is doing now rather than a pile of readings from this morning.

User management

Open from the dashboard. Search by name or device code, choose a role, press and hold. The same screen holds the 4-digit PIN and the access code, and the switch that turns the access code off.

Readiness and coverage

READY checks the things that must be true before a storm: a protocol for every purok and signal, the translations, the volunteers, the residents registered against the household count, and that every centre has a location. Each gap has a Fix that goes straight to the screen that closes it.

Documents saved on that screen are readable with no signal, once the screen has been opened once with a connection.

7 · Before the storm season

Work through this once, in fair weather. Every item is something only a person who knows the barangay can do.

Do thisWhy it matters
Change the PIN and the access codeThe ones the system was set up with are known to everyone who saw it being built.
Grant the real rolesEach official's and volunteer's own phone, by device code. Remove any left over from testing.
Enter namesA phone with no name on ME cannot file a hazard or flood report. Officials especially.
Confirm the evacuation centresNames, locations, and true capacities — the capacity is what triggers the FULL warning.
Enter the household countUntil it is entered, readiness cannot say whether enough residents are registered.
Check the boundary and the routesWalk one route on the map against the real street. The map is built from OpenStreetMap, which can be wrong or out of date.
Check the purok protocolsEvery purok, every signal, in the language residents read.
Have everyone install the appOnce, with a connection, so the map and wording are on the phone before they are needed.
Rehearse one advisoryIssue Signal 1 and lift it, so the officials have used the control before the night they must.

8 · When something is wrong

What you seeWhat it means and what to do
The map is a plain drawing with no colours The phone could not fetch the street map, so it is showing the barangay's own drawn map. Every street, route and pin is still correct. It will come back with the connection.
CACHED 6 HOURS AGO No connection since then. What is shown is six hours old — treat it as such, and do not assume no news is good news.
Something stuck in WAITING TO SEND Normal with no signal. If it persists with a good connection, open the item and read the reason.
A report is refused Most often there is no name on that phone's ME screen. Enter one and send again.
The official pages will not open Either the phone has not been made an official in User Management, or the PIN is wrong. The PIN alone never grants access.
Too many wrong codes Entry is locked for ten minutes on that phone. This is deliberate.
The camera will not scan Either permission was refused or the page is not on a secure connection. Use manual entry — it records exactly the same thing.
The SOS button does nothing visible It has still been recorded. Look for SENT and the waiting time. If the phone is offline it is held and will go automatically.
A screen shows raw words like ui.signal_no The wording has not reached this phone yet. Open it once with a connection.

Reporting a fault

Note which screen, what the strip at the top said, and whether the phone had a signal. Those three things are usually enough to find it.

What this app is not

SalbaBayan does not replace PAGASA, the municipal disaster office, or the emergency telephone numbers. It carries one barangay's own warning, its own map and its own rescue calls, and it is built so that those keep working when the network does not.
